About

DMX for Gamers started out as a small project to add lighting for a Scorched3D (http://www.scorched3d.co.uk/) LAN party. The server for Scorched3D is a console application with outputs that show significant events (game starting, player killed, game win). These outputs were used to trigger lights over DMX.

The addition of lighting to the LAN party was a hit. As time allowed, work was done to make the application more generic and user friendly.

This is certainly not the most sophisticated DMX application out there. The hope is that it fills a 'niche' and that others find ways to use this utility in their LAN parties with other games or maybe in completely new ways.

Enjoy!

Introduction

Users of this software are assumed to have basic knowledge of electronic devices and how to wire them.

DMX512 (Digital Multiplex or DMX for short) is a standard for digital communications used to control stage lighting and effects. It's been around a long time, but is simple and very reliable.

A series of 512 bytes of data is constantly sent over a shared bus line. Each of these bytes is a 'channel' and starts with channel 1. The last channel being 512.

Devices (like lights) on the bus line are assigned addresses. The address is what 'channel' the device needs to look at. Devices can and most likely will look at several channels. So a 3-channel light assigned to address 4 will look at channels 4, 5 and 6.

Hardware Requirements

First and foremost you need a PC to run the software. DMX for Gamers itself does not require much horsepower. Any PC capable of running Windows 7 or higher should be sufficient.

To actually drive lights with DMX for Gamers you will require the following:

  • DMX Adapter

    • Tested and Works
      • Enttec Open DMX USB
      • DTECH USB to RS422/RS485
      • ArtNet (DMX over IP)
    • Works in Theory
      • Any FTDI based DMX adapter
      • Any RS485 adapter

  • Any DMX controllable lights with manuals

    • DMX lights come in all styles and functionalities. I would recommend a simple 3 or 4 channel light to get started. You can find cheap LED DMX lights from $40 - $60 online.
    • You will need manuals or DMX instructions for the lights in order to use them with DMX for Gamers.

  • DMX cords

    • By the specs, DMX cables are 5 pins. However a lot of hardware uses 3 pin cables.
    • For short runs, standard 3 pin XLR audio cables will work.
    • For short runs you should not need to worry about termination. If you don't know what termination is, don't worry about it.
